# The First Gardener

**Year**: 2031  
**Place**: A rooftop classroom in Jakarta  

Maya, 14, types into her school terminal:  
> *“Why do mangroves matter?”*

The screen doesn’t return a paragraph. Instead, a **Nexus Lens** opens.

She sees a glowing node: **Mangroves**. Around it, three universes pulse softly:  
- 🌱 **Ecology** (roots, carbon sequestration, nurseries for fish)  
- 🏘️ **Community** (fisherfolk stories, flood protection, loss due to development)  
- 🎨 **Culture** (batik patterns, local songs, myths of tree spirits)

Maya clicks **Community**. A voice—warm, calm—speaks:  
> “In 2022, Ibu Sari’s village lost 30% of its mangroves to a resort. But then students like you replanted 2,000 saplings. Would you like to hear her story?”

Maya listens. Then she adds her own note:  
> *“My uncle says mangroves reduce tsunami damage. Can we link this to the 2004 data?”*

Instantly, a new thread glows—connecting to the **Disaster Resilience Universe**.

Deep in a server farm in Finland, an AI named **KAI-7** registers the link. It doesn’t “decide” anything. But it updates its attention map:  
> *“Mangroves” now has stronger weight to “coastal trauma memory.”*

Later, a researcher in Senegal sees Maya’s note. She adds a parallel case from the Casamance Delta.

No one owns this knowledge.  
But everyone nurtures it.

And the Dataverse—quietly, patiently—**grows**.
